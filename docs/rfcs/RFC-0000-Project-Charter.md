# Project Charter

## Abstract

Chronicle is an open-source framework for persistent tabletop role-playing campaigns directed through Narrative Intelligence.

The framework separates storytelling from truth: Narrative Intelligence tells the story, while Chronicle preserves the campaign, its rules, its characters, its memories, and its current state.

The official Chronicle application is the first implementation of this framework. The architecture is designed for future growth, but delivery remains intentionally limited to the requirements of the current milestone.

## 1. Purpose

Chronicle exists to make persistent tabletop role-playing experiences available to players who want to play without depending on the continuous availability of a human Game Master or a complete player group.

Chronicle MUST provide more than a conversational interface. It MUST preserve continuity across sessions, maintain the authoritative state of the campaign, execute deterministic game mechanics, and direct Narrative Intelligence through explicit context and structured contracts.

The experience SHOULD resemble a tabletop campaign conducted by an experienced Game Master:

- characters remember what they have lived;
- non-player characters react consistently to past interactions;
- actions produce lasting consequences;
- rules are applied predictably;
- dramatic tests interrupt the narrative until their results are known;
- campaigns remain coherent over long periods.

## 2. Product Identity

Chronicle is a **persistent narrative engine framework for tabletop role-playing games**.

Chronicle is not defined by a specific AI provider, user interface, programming language, database, or RPG system.

The official application is one delivery of the framework, not the framework itself.

Chronicle approaches interactive storytelling as a directed production composed of specialized roles:

- the **Chronicle Director** coordinates the active campaign structure and prepares the context of play;
- the **Narrator** transforms curated campaign state into narrative;
- the **Archivist** preserves the memories and consequences created by play;
- deterministic engines execute rules, randomness, persistence, and state transitions.

These names represent architectural responsibilities, not autonomous product features.

## 3. Core Philosophy

Chronicle separates **storytelling** from **truth**.

Narrative output is not the authoritative state of the campaign.

The Chronicle Core owns the authoritative state. Narrative Intelligence receives a temporary, curated representation of that state and produces structured narrative output.

The fundamental relationship is:

```text
Campaign Truth
      ↓
Curated Context
      ↓
Narrative Intelligence
      ↓
Structured Narrative
      ↓
Validated State Transition
```

Narrative Intelligence MAY propose actions, outcomes, memories, or transitions.

Chronicle MUST validate and persist every permanent change through application-controlled logic.

## 4. Problem Statement

AI-driven tabletop RPG experiences commonly degrade over time because the same model is expected to narrate, remember, apply rules, manage characters, preserve continuity, and invent new content simultaneously.

This frequently produces:

- contradictory character information;
- forgotten relationships;
- fabricated rules;
- inconsistent inventory or attributes;
- disappearing or redefined NPCs;
- campaign plots that lose direction;
- excessive context growth;
- dependence on a single model or provider;
- random outcomes generated by narrative text rather than game logic.

Chronicle addresses these problems by assigning persistent knowledge and deterministic responsibilities to the application rather than to Narrative Intelligence.

## 5. Product Goals

Chronicle MUST enable a player to create and continue a persistent tabletop RPG campaign across multiple sessions.

The initial product SHOULD allow the player to:

- choose a supported RPG system;
- configure campaign-specific narrative preferences;
- create and persist a player character;
- generate a planned campaign structure;
- create persistent NPCs before they enter the visible narrative;
- play sessions through an interactive narrative interface;
- pause the narrative at moments that require a test;
- execute dice rolls through deterministic application logic;
- continue narration from the result of the test;
- preserve session history;
- produce campaign memories with relevance and lifetime;
- update character progression and relationships after a session;
- continue an unfinished session without losing state.

The product SHOULD make the player feel that the campaign remembers what was lived, not merely what was written in the latest conversation.

## 6. Framework Goals

The framework architecture MUST allow RPG systems to be added without redesigning the Chronicle Core.

A supported RPG system SHOULD provide system-specific knowledge and configuration, including:

- character sheet structure;
- rules and terminology;
- dice mechanics;
- validation rules;
- system-specific narrative guidance;
- system-specific retrieval content.

The domain MUST depend on a system-level abstraction such as a `RuleSet`, not on a specific retrieval implementation.

RAG, embeddings, document stores, local files, and external services are infrastructure concerns and MUST NOT become domain concepts.

## 7. Initial Delivery Scope

The first delivery is intentionally constrained.

The MVP includes:

- one local user;
- single-player campaigns;
- the official Chronicle application;
- one active player character per campaign;
- persistent campaigns and sessions;
- structured character sheets using field-and-value data;
- one RPG system for development and validation;
- Werewolf: The Apocalypse as the initial Rule Set;
- one Narrative Intelligence provider implementation;
- deterministic dice execution;
- campaign planning with Acts and Scenes;
- persistent NPC generation;
- Chronicle Director orchestration;
- Narrator integration;
- Archivist-based session finalization;
- campaign memories with relevance and lifetime;
- session chat history;
- local persistent storage;
- a desktop-first user experience.

The MVP MAY use provider-specific libraries internally, but provider-specific behavior MUST remain behind an abstraction.

## 8. Explicit Non-Goals for the MVP

The MVP MUST NOT be delayed by the implementation of:

- multiplayer sessions;
- mobile, tablet, television, or console clients;
- synchronized cloud campaigns;
- live streaming integrations;
- voice input or voice narration;
- dynamic image generation;
- dynamic avatar generation;
- generated maps;
- animated dice;
- automated music playback;
- multiple simultaneous player characters;
- online matchmaking;
- marketplace or plug-in distribution;
- visual campaign editors;
- public campaign hosting;
- multiple Narrative Intelligence providers exposed to end users.

The architecture MAY preserve extension points for these capabilities.

Their future possibility MUST NOT create implementation work unless required by the current milestone.

## 9. Narrative Structure

Chronicle uses the following narrative hierarchy:

```text
Campaign
└── Session
    └── Act
        └── Scene
```

### Campaign

The complete persistent role-playing chronicle.

The Campaign is the ownership boundary for characters, NPCs, relationships, memories, sessions, narrative plans, assets, and campaign state.

### Session

A continuous period of play started and ended by the player.

A Session MAY contain multiple Acts.

### Act

A major dramatic division of a Session.

An Act groups Scenes that contribute to the same major narrative movement, objective, conflict, or dramatic phase.

Example:

```text
Act: The Desert War
```

### Scene

The smallest directed unit of active narrative context.

A Scene isolates the location, participants, immediate objective, active conflict, and information required for the current interaction.

Example:

```text
Act: The Desert War
├── Scene: Battle at the Northern Gate
├── Scene: Defense of the Fortress
└── Scene: Assault on the Hill
```

Characters participating in one Scene MUST NOT be assumed to participate in another Scene.

The Chronicle Director MUST use the active Scene as a primary context boundary.

## 10. Campaign Memory

Chronicle treats remembered consequences as first-class domain information.

A campaign memory represents something that remains meaningful after it was lived.

A memory MAY include:

- a description;
- involved characters;
- the Session, Act, and Scene where it originated;
- narrative importance;
- current relevance;
- lifetime measured in sessions;
- permanent or temporary status;
- emotional meaning;
- known consequences;
- entities that remember it.

Temporary memories MAY lose relevance over time.

Permanent memories MUST remain part of the campaign history.

Expired memories SHOULD be archived rather than destroyed when auditability or campaign history requires preservation.

The exact distinction between campaign-wide memory and character-specific memory will be defined by the Domain Model RFC.

## 11. Chronicle Director

The Chronicle Director is the orchestration role responsible for preparing and advancing the playable structure of the campaign.

The Chronicle Director MUST NOT narrate prose, roll dice, directly mutate character sheets, or become the source of campaign truth.

The Chronicle Director is responsible for coordinating:

- the active Campaign;
- the active Session;
- the active Act;
- the active Scene;
- Scene participants;
- immediate objectives;
- relevant campaign memories;
- relevant character state;
- relevant Rule Set knowledge;
- context assembly requests;
- structured Narrator responses;
- pending dice tests;
- continuation after resolved tests;
- Scene and Act transitions.

The Chronicle Director directs the production.

The Narrator tells the story.

## 12. Dice and Dramatic Interruption

Narrative Intelligence MUST NOT generate random results.

When a test is required, the Narrator MUST return a structured roll request.

The application MUST stop narrative progression at that point and present the test to the player.

The player explicitly triggers the roll.

Chronicle then:

1. executes the random function;
2. applies the active Rule Set;
3. persists the roll and its result;
4. sends the validated result back to the Narrator;
5. resumes the narrative from the interrupted moment.

The initial delivery MAY present a simple roll button and textual result.

Animated dice are outside the MVP.

## 13. Campaign Creation

Campaign creation SHOULD follow this flow:

1. The player creates a new Campaign.
2. The player selects a Rule Set.
3. The player optionally enters narrative customizations.
4. The player fills the character sheet.
5. Chronicle validates and persists the character data according to the active delivery capabilities.
6. Narrative Intelligence evaluates the character, Rule Set, and customizations.
7. Chronicle creates persistent NPC candidates with varied power levels.
8. Chronicle creates general campaign metadata.
9. Chronicle creates an initial narrative plan divided into Acts and Scenes.
10. Chronicle persists the result as campaign data that is hidden or visible according to its purpose.

NPCs created during campaign initialization exist before their first appearance in the visible story.

## 14. Session Lifecycle

A Session begins when the player starts a new Session or continues an unfinished one.

During play, the system repeatedly performs:

```text
Player Input
     ↓
Chronicle Director
     ↓
Campaign Context Selection
     ↓
Rule Set Retrieval
     ↓
Prompt Construction
     ↓
Narrator
     ↓
Structured Response Validation
     ↓
Narrative Rendering or Pending Roll
```

A Session ends only when the player explicitly requests finalization or when an equivalent controlled workflow is executed.

At Session finalization, the Archivist SHOULD evaluate the completed Session and propose:

- new campaign memories;
- updates to existing memories;
- relevance and lifetime changes;
- character progression;
- relationship changes;
- newly known information;
- character state changes;
- a Session summary.

Chronicle MUST validate and persist the accepted changes.

Non-permanent memories SHOULD age according to their lifetime policy.

Permanent memories SHOULD accumulate age without losing their permanent status.

## 15. Target Audience

The primary audience is:

- tabletop RPG players who want to play without requiring a regularly available group or human Game Master;
- players who value persistent, character-focused, long-form campaigns.

The secondary audience is:

- open-source contributors;
- developers interested in persistent narrative applications;
- developers creating additional Rule Sets or alternative Chronicle clients.

## 16. Success Criteria

The first delivery is successful when a player can:

- create a Werewolf: The Apocalypse campaign;
- complete multiple Sessions;
- continue an unfinished Session;
- perform deterministic dice tests;
- observe persistent character and NPC continuity;
- finish Sessions with updated memories and progression;
- return later without significant contradictions in campaign state.

Narrative quality alone is not sufficient.

Persistence, consistency, recoverability, and deterministic rule execution are part of the product definition.

## 17. Delivery Discipline

Chronicle distinguishes between two horizons.

### Architecture Horizon

The architecture may anticipate future clients, multiplayer play, additional Rule Sets, alternative Narrative Intelligence providers, multimedia, streaming, and other integrations.

### Delivery Horizon

Only capabilities required by the active milestone are implemented.

A future capability MUST NOT enter the current implementation solely because the architecture can support it.

Chronicle is designed for the future.

The official Chronicle application is built for today.

## 18. Technology Neutrality

This Charter does not select:

- a programming language;
- a UI framework;
- a persistence technology;
- a vector database;
- a deployment platform;
- an AI provider;
- a serialization library;
- an API style.

Those decisions belong to dedicated RFCs and ADRs.

No implementation choice may redefine the product identity established by this Charter.

## 19. Long-Term Direction

Chronicle is intended to remain useful as its implementation evolves.

If Chronicle later supports multiplayer campaigns, this Charter remains valid.

If Chronicle later supports voice, images, music, streaming, or multiple clients, this Charter remains valid.

If Chronicle later replaces LLM-based narration with another form of Narrative Intelligence, this Charter remains valid.

Chronicle is defined by the separation between persistent campaign truth and generated narrative, not by the technology used to produce that narrative.

## 20. Final Principle

The Narrator tells the story.

The Chronicle Director directs the experience.

The Archivist preserves what was lived.

Chronicle remembers.
